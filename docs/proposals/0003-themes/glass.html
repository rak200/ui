All four Default High contrast Matrix Glass

Glass: translucent raised surfaces, over a page that is not flat

Question 2's recommendation. The raised surfaces — the cards, the toasts, the tip and the dialog — read a translucent colour and a backdrop filter; the surface itself stays opaque, so the fields inside a card are as legible as anywhere else. The theme also moves two grounds: a neutral slate accent, and the text in pure black and pure white. The page behind follows the scheme, as a host's would.

The glass here is chosen by eye — white in the light, black in the dark, both at 0.20, and a 10px blur — so 80% of the page comes through. What it lets through is what everything resting on it is read against: not the text alone, but the error message, a checked box, a switch that is off and the focus ring, which have less room to give than the text has.

The first row on the glass is the page it can take: the lightest grey behind it in the dark scheme, the darkest in the light, before one of the five fails. A page that does not follow the scheme — white behind dark glass, black behind light — is not measured, because no host following the scheme draws one.

The controls can be made translucent too, each group on its own: their fills at an opacity, the neutral ones and the accent ones apart, every edge still opaque. A neutral fill only adds to the glass beneath it, so text on a field, a secondary button or a row reads at least as well as on the glass or the surface, whichever is worse — the rows above bound it. An accent fill is different, and the checked controls and the primary button each have their own opacity: a checked box is read against the glass around it, and a button's label and its hover against the fill itself.

To see a reading rather than read it, put one colour behind every card: the limit itself, or one of the reference's colours. The rows under the panel then measure each of the five over exactly that page. Around 3:1 many readers still read comfortably; 4.5:1 is the margin kept for those who cannot, and for glare and poor screens.
